How OpenJS Foundation Took Over jQuery Governance

The governance of jQuery shifted to the OpenJS Foundation through a series of strategic foundation mergers designed to unify the JavaScript ecosystem. Originally managed by its creator and early contributors, jQuery eventually formed the jQuery Foundation, which merged with the Dojo Foundation in 2016 to establish the JS Foundation. In 2019, the JS Foundation combined with the Node.js Foundation to form the OpenJS Foundation, officially transferring jQuery’s institutional, legal, and operational oversight under the new organization.

The Early Years and the jQuery Foundation

When John Resig launched jQuery in 2006, it quickly became the dominant client-side library on the web. As adoption expanded, maintaining the library required a formal organizational structure to handle intellectual property, licensing, and community contributions. In 2012, the jQuery team established the jQuery Foundation as an independent, non-profit organization to oversee jQuery and complementary projects like jQuery UI, jQuery Mobile, and QUnit. This foundation ensured the software remained open-source, vendor-neutral, and financially supported.

Transition to the JS Foundation (2016)

By 2016, the scope of web development had diversified, and hosting individual foundations for single technologies became redundant. To foster better collaboration across the client-side JavaScript community, the jQuery Foundation merged with the Dojo Foundation to create the JS Foundation, supported as a collaborative project under the Linux Foundation. Through this move, jQuery's intellectual property, trademark assets, and governance frameworks transitioned to the broader JS Foundation umbrella.

The Merger into the OpenJS Foundation (2019)

In 2019, the JavaScript community took another major consolidation step. The JS Foundation (focused predominantly on client-side projects) and the Node.js Foundation (focused on server-side runtimes) announced a formal merger to establish the OpenJS Foundation. Supported by industry leaders and the Linux Foundation, this consolidation eliminated duplicated operational efforts and provided a unified home for critical JavaScript infrastructure.

Through this merger, all projects governed by the JS Foundation, including jQuery, were automatically transferred to the OpenJS Foundation.

How jQuery Is Governed Today

Under the OpenJS Foundation, jQuery operates as an "At-Large" or "Impact" project. The governance operates under a bi-level model:

  • Technical Direction: Day-to-day code maintenance, release management, and architectural roadmaps remain entirely with the independent jQuery Core Team and its Project Management Committee (PMC).
  • Legal and Administrative Support: The OpenJS Foundation manages trademarks, code copyrights, legal protections, infrastructure hosting, and community event management.

This transition relieved the jQuery maintainers of administrative overhead while preserving the library's technical autonomy under a secure, neutral non-profit foundation.
